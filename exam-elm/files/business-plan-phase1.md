## 3. The top 5 business opportunities

Each: demand evidence, competitors, startup + maintenance costs (money and time, honest), monetization, risks.

### Opportunity 1 — Free exam-prep toolkit hub ✅ STRONGEST RECOMMENDATION
*Revision timetable generator, exam countdown + daily-hours planner, syllabus coverage tracker, past-paper organizer, grade calculators, free template generators. No signup, no install, no paywall. South-Asia-first defaults (CBSE/NEB/VTU/Mumbai board formulas, NEB chapter lists), global modes.*

- **Demand evidence:** 30 documented problems converge on planning/grades/exam-readiness; students literally ask strangers to build timetables (P6); "what do I need on my final" is the most-searched grade question every finals season; 6 TARGET keywords in this cluster; the #1 underserved need is a personal revision-timetable builder.
- **Competitors:** page-1 held by small indie tools, no dominant brand; closest analog globaledpath.com is template-heavy, tool-light. **The syllabus coverage tracker has no web incumbent at all.** Single-purpose tools (Pomofocus, gpacalculator.net) can't match an integrated ecosystem.
- **Startup cost:** $0 money; ~2–4 weeks of build time for the first 3 tools (static site + client-side JS + localStorage; no backend, no AI costs). Free hosting (GitHub/Netlify/Cloudflare Pages) + free .com.np domain possible.
- **Maintenance:** VERY LOW — evergreen tools; *estimate* 0–1 hr/week steady state; update only when boards change grading rules.
- **Monetization:** AdSense from day one (Nepal wire verified) → affiliates on guide pages (Coursera 15–45%, Bookshop.org 10%, Amazon 4.5%) → Mediavine Journey at 1,000 sessions → Paddle digital products month 6+ ($3–$15 band: planners, cheat-sheet packs, Notion dashboard). Shareable result links ("I need 96%") create a viral loop.
- **Risks:** seasonal finals spikes (mitigated: evergreen planners + South-Asia exam calendars); keyword verdicts are estimates (re-validate with Search Console in Phase 4); Paddle application review is the gating risk for products (but nothing depends on it for 6+ months).
- **Score: 20/20** on your priority order.

### Opportunity 2 — Grade/GPA calculator micro-site
*Multi-course final-grade calculator, multi-board CGPA↔percentage converter, weighted GPA, edge-case GPA (retakes/W's).*

- **Demand evidence:** SDN GPA-confusion threads are evergreen; South-Asia board-formula confusion (CBSE ×9.5 vs VTU (CGPA−0.75)×10 vs Mumbai Univ) drives repeat queries; keywords #2, #3, #17 all TARGET.
- **Competitors:** head "gpa calculator" crowded (giants + niche sites); long-tails held by small indie sites — winnable with better UX (multi-course view, board switcher).
- **Startup cost:** $0; the simplest tools to build (~1–2 weeks).
- **Maintenance:** NEGLIGIBLE — calculators don't rot.
- **Monetization:** WEAK — ads only, thin affiliate fit, thin products. This is why it can't be the standalone business.
- **Risks:** narrow (5–6 calculators, then done); crowded head term; seasonal.
- **Score: 16/20 → best as the flagship calculator section inside Opportunity 1.**

### Opportunity 3 — Nepal NEB exam-prep platform (the pivot candidate)
*Topic-tagged NEB past-paper bank, written step-by-step solutions, NEB worksheet/question generator; SEE / +2 / IOE / Lok Sewa.*

- **Demand evidence:** massive and observed — Gurukul Nepal's SEE maths model-solutions video at **656,236 views**, Nepali grammar video at **698,954**; "Loksewa Tayari Offline" app at **290k installs**; paid rivals prove willingness to pay (MyAbhyaash IOE/CEE Rs 950/yr; Quizmandu Lok Sewa NPR 1,499 lifetime; Veedhyapeeth Rs 2,500).
- **Competitors:** a dozen free SEO blogs (EducateNepal, Bidhya Mandir) with raw paper archives but no solutions and terrible discovery; Sajha Notes (25 solved papers, science-skewed); YouTube channels (unskimmable video solutions); free apps (ad-heavy, stale). **Nobody offers a topic-tagged NEB question bank** — paperguides.org proves the format (generator + per-topic index) but covers **zero NEB** (A Levels + Kathmandu Univ only; potential collaborator).
- **Startup cost:** $0 money (free .com.np domain verified via register.com.np; free static hosting); the *question generator* is pure software (~2–3 weeks). The solved-paper library is the expensive part: one full paper ≈ 2–4 hrs expert work.
- **Maintenance:** HIGH — *estimate* 6–10 hrs/week in exam season (Feb–May) collecting/organizing each cycle's papers; 1–2 hrs/week off-season; solutions need a correction workflow.
- **Monetization:** AdSense day one (only $0, no-paperwork rail). **Domestic wallets are NOT day-one:** eSewa merchant and Khalti merchant both require a registered business + PAN/VAT + tax clearance (verified from official pages) — a paperwork project for later. Nepali-audience ad rates are low; honestly a "thousands per year at scale" business, not venture-scale.
- **Risks:** **NEB paper copyright could not be verified** (assume the board owns them; takedown risk low but nonzero — mitigate with transcribed/original practice questions + instant takedown readiness); **ICAN (CA Nepal) papers are explicitly off-limits** (ICAN forbids republication); extreme seasonality (dead Jun–Dec); free competition is the default (YouTube/Facebook/TikTok drops); single-country ceiling.
- **Score: 14/20.** The strongest pivot evaluated — and it loses on your own priorities: revenue gated behind registration paperwork, brutal seasonality, and a content treadmill that conflicts with minimal maintenance. **Its best insights are absorbed into Opportunity 1** (NEB/South-Asia-first wedge from day one); the full NEB bank is reserved as a Phase 6+ expansion once revenue funds the registration paperwork.

### Opportunity 4 — Templates/printables + digital products engine
*Free template generators (Cornell notes, semester planner, essay word-budget planner) as lead magnets → Paddle digital products.*

- **Demand evidence:** Etsy proves willingness to pay ($7–$20 observed for study planners); "free, no email" beats gated blogs (keyword TARGET); templates are the #1 observed student digital product category ($3–$15 band).
- **Competitors:** paid (Etsy/Notion sellers), email-gated (blogs), or uncurated (Notion's 6,813-template gallery) — the free-download lane is nearly empty.
- **Startup cost:** $0; PDFs + print CSS + simple generators (~2 weeks).
- **Maintenance:** LOW — templates are evergreen; *estimate* <1 hr/week.
- **Monetization:** STRONG FIT — Paddle verified Nepal-eligible; proposed products pegged to observed bands (Notion Study OS $12–15, exam sprint planner $5–7, cheat-sheet packs $4–6); templates double as newsletter bait.
- **Risks:** easily copied (defensibility comes from generators, not static PDFs); weaker search demand than tools — it has no traffic engine of its own.
- **Score: 14/20 → ideal pillar and monetization engine inside Opportunity 1.**

### Opportunity 5 — Exam-oriented subject study guides
*Worked-example STEM/engineering guides, formula guides, past-paper frequency analyses — the method you use yourself.*

- **Demand evidence:** "studying hard but failing" is a constant genre; uni-math pain is weekly on r/GetStudying and r/EngineeringStudents.
- **Competitors:** guide head terms held by universities/publishers (avoid — long-tail grind required); but the *exam-oriented, worked-example* gap is real (SparkNotes lit-only; OpenStax textbook-dense, not exam-oriented).
- **Startup cost:** $0 money, HIGH time cost — each guide is hours of expert work; slowest path to traffic.
- **Maintenance:** MEDIUM — content stays fresh, but output pace is the bottleneck for a solo student-builder.
- **Monetization:** GOOD — affiliates fit guide pages (Bookshop.org 10%, Coursera 15–45%); cheat-sheet packs are natural Paddle products.
- **Risks:** highest production burden alongside your own studies; authority competition.
- **Score: 12/20 → supporting long-tail content (formula guides paired with tools), not the business.**

### Evaluated and rejected (one line each)
- **Newsletter-first study business (10/20):** best maintenance of the rejected set and a fine *audience layer on top of tools*, but education sponsors pay the bargain bin ($7.21/1,000 subs) and cadence death kills most student newsletters.
- **Study accountability platform (8/20):** real demand, but a moderation/safety operation (minors, harassment, 1–3 hrs/day forever) wearing a tool costume — fails minimal-maintenance catastrophically.
- **Study-skills blog (7/20):** biggest raw demand, worst everything else — creator-dominated, undifferentiated, monetizes poorly.
- **Creator-led exam-prep brand (6/20):** 8–12+ hrs/week treadmill for ~$0.50–$2 CPMs; directly competes with your studies.

It loses on your stated priorities, in order: (a) revenue is *gated* (no payment acceptance until business registration; low domestic ad rates; dead half the year) vs. the toolkit's verified day-one revenue with zero paperwork; (b) differentiation is real but narrower; (c) its best wedge is itself tool-shaped — which the toolkit already is; (d) it demands 6–15 hrs/week of content labor vs. the toolkit's ~0–1 hr/week. The honest sizing: Nepal-first is a "thousands per year at scale" business with a paperwork prerequisite; the toolkit is a global, evergreen, zero-paperwork asset. Following the evidence, not sentiment.

## 5. What makes it different — 10 features students would love

1. **No signup, no install, no email wall — ever.** The anti-pattern of every competitor; "no signup" is itself a searched phrase.
2. **South-Asia-first, global by default.** Board switchers (CBSE ×9.5, VTU, Mumbai Univ, 4.0), NEB chapter lists and exam presets — while every competitor assumes the US. (This is the Nepal opportunity's wedge, captured without its risks.)
3. **Exam-date-aware everything.** Enter exam dates once; every tool plans backwards from them.
4. **Syllabus coverage tracker.** "How much of the syllabus have I finished?" — the #1 anxiety question with zero good free web answer. Blue ocean.
5. **Personal past-paper organizer.** Log questions by topic/year, mark attempted/confident, see weakest topics — student-side, where existing banks are teacher tools. (Format proven by paperguides.org; NEB version = Phase 6+ expansion.)
6. **One integrated toolkit, not 6 tabs.** Countdown → daily hours → timetable → syllabus tracker → pomodoro sessions auto-log to the study logger. The moat is the ecosystem.
7. **Spaced repetition built into planning.** Auto-placed 1/3/7/14-day review blocks in every generated timetable.
8. **Shareable result links.** "I need 96% on my final" as a link for the study group — a viral loop no grade calculator has.
9. **Show-your-work trust.** Every guide cites sources; every method shows its working (like the frequency-count analyses). The trust playbook of the best competitors, at zero price — plus a correction workflow (Sajha Notes' report-button model).
10. **Fast, light, accessible.** Low-bandwidth design for the audience competitors ignore — loads fast on cheap phones and slow connections; offline-capable PWA beats Nepal's ad-heavy app graveyard.

## 8. Risks (honest)

- **Seasonality:** tool traffic spikes around finals — mitigated by evergreen planners + Southern-hemisphere/South-Asia exam calendars.
- **Estimates, not measurements:** keyword verdicts rest on page-1 composition (Trends was rate-limited); Reddit direct access was blocked (mirror + snippets used, disclosed). Re-validate with real Search Console data in Phase 4.
- **Paddle review risk:** the one gating risk for digital products — but nothing depends on it for 6+ months.
- **Copycats:** any single tool can be cloned; the defense is the integrated ecosystem + no-signup UX + South-Asia defaults.
- **Scope creep toward Nepal content:** the NEB bank is tempting but it's a content treadmill with copyright gray zones — fenced to Phase 6+ by design, not Phase 2.
- **Your time:** you're a student first. The plan is deliberately backend-free and AI-cost-free so progress survives busy weeks; weekly writing/moderation treadmills were explicitly rejected for this reason.
