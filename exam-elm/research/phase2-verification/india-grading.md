## 1. CBSE (Central Board of Secondary Education)

### Historical CGPA→percentage (CCE era, 2010–2017)
- **Formula (VERIFIED, official): Percentage = CGPA × 9.5**; subject-wise **Indicative % = 9.5 × subject grade point**.
- **Official source:** CBSE Circular No. 24/2010, dated 28-05-2010. It set "overall indicative percentage of
  marks = 9.5 × CGPA of the candidate" and "indicative percentage of marks = 9.5 × GP of the subject."
  The result is explicitly an *indicative* percentage, not exact marks.
  Sources: http://www.icbse.com/cgpa ; https://gradekar.com/blog/cgpa-to-percentage-guide
- **Origin of 9.5:** CBSE took five years of board results, found the average marks of candidates scoring in
  the 91–100 (A1) band was close to 95; 95 ÷ 10 (grade point) = 9.5. A 10 CGPA therefore equals 95%, not 100%.
  Source: http://www.icbse.com/cgpa
- **Grade→points table (CCE era, VERIFIED, corroborated by 4+ sources):** A1=10, A2=9, B1=8, B2=7, C1=6,
  C2=5, D=4, E1/E2=fail (0). Indicative mark ranges: A1 91–100, A2 81–90, B1 71–80, B2 61–70, C1 51–60,
  C2 41–50, D 33–40.
  Sources: http://www.icbse.com/cgpa ; https://www.pw.live/news/how-to-convert-cgpa-to-percentage-cbse-class-10-formula-calculator-2026 ; https://www.legalang.org/tools/education/cgpa-calculator-formula.html
- **CGPA construction (VERIFIED by multiple sources):** CGPA = average of grade points of the **five main
  subjects**; a designated additional (6th) subject is **excluded** from the CGPA.
  Sources: http://www.icbse.com/cgpa ; https://www.shiksha.com/studyabroad/what-is-cgpa-articlepage-154643
  (Class 12 CGPA calculation strictly excludes additional/sixth subjects).
- **Historical pass rule (CCE era):** minimum grade D (≥33%) in each of the five subjects.
  Source: https://www.pw.live/news/how-to-convert-cgpa-to-percentage-cbse-class-10-formula-calculator-2026
  (E1/E2 = fail; must clear compartment to reach D/33%).

### Current status — CCE is DISCONTINUED (VERIFIED)
- CBSE **abolished CCE in March 2017** (circular dated 21.03.2017, reported 22.03.2017) and introduced the
  "Uniform System of Assessment, Examination and Report Card," restoring marks-based assessment (pen-paper
  tests ~90% weight) from the 2017–18 academic year; Class 10 board exams restored from 2018.
  Sources: https://www.india.com/education/cbse-new-mandate-does-away-with-cce-introduces-uniform-assessment-from-class-vi-onwards-1949084/?d=dds ; https://www.careerindia.com/news/cbse-schools-get-new-format-assessment-old-system-scrapped-018982.html ; http://www.icbse.com/cgpa
- **Current official position:** CBSE Class 10 and 12 marksheets show **subject marks + positional grades,
  no CGPA, no grade points, no ×9.5 conversion** on the sheet. In each subject, passing students are ranked
  and split into eight equal groups: top 1/8th = A1, next = A2 … D2; non-pass = E (Essential Repeat).
  CBSE does **not** declare an aggregate percentage, division, distinction, or merit list.
  Source: https://supertutor.in/resources/blog/cbse-grading-system-2026/ (based on CBSE Secondary & Senior
  Secondary Curriculum 2026–27 on cbseacademic.nic.in)
- **Pass today:** 33% in each subject (Class 10: five main subjects; Class 12: each subject incl. separate
  theory/practical where applicable); compartment allowed in up to 2 subjects (Class 12).
  Source: https://supertutor.in/resources/blog/cbse-grading-system-2026/
- **Implication for StudyPilot:** ×9.5 applies ONLY to pre-2018 CCE-era Class 10 (and Classes 9/12 CGPA-era)
  marksheets still in circulation. Label it "CBSE (old CGPA marksheets only)". Current marksheets need no
  conversion — use the printed marks. Do NOT show ×9.5 as CBSE's current rule.
- **UNVERIFIED:** whether CBSE's current curriculum documents still contain any ×9.5 text (could not open
  cbse.gov.in/cbseacademic.nic.in; secondary sources say no). CBSE grace-mark and revaluation specifics
  (exact mark caps/fees) were not researched — UNVERIFIED.

## 2. ICSE (Class 10) / ISC (Class 12) — CISCE

### ICSE Class 10 (VERIFIED by 4+ corroborating sources; primary CISCE circular not directly retrieved)
- **No CGPA. Percentage = (total marks of best five subjects ÷ 500) × 100.**
- **English Language is compulsorily included** among the five; CISCE selects the combination giving the
  highest aggregate (lowest other subject is dropped when 6+ subjects are taken).
  Sources: https://collegedunia.com/exams/icse-class-x-board/grading-system ; https://www.getmyuni.com/articles/how-to-calculate-icse-percentage ; https://www.shiksha.com/boards/icse-board-results
- Subject marks feeding the average: English = average of English Language + Literature in English; Science =
  average of Physics/Chemistry/Biology; Social Science = average of History & Civics/Geography.
  Source: https://www.shiksha.com/boards/icse-board-results
- **External grade scale (1–9):** 1–2 = Outstanding, 3–5 = Credit, 6–7 = Pass, 8–9 = Fail. Internal
  assessment graded A–E (A–D recorded on certificate).
  Source: https://www.boardingschoolsofindia.com/icse-board
- **Pass:** minimum **33% in each subject** (written and internal assessed separately).
  Sources: https://collegedunia.com/exams/icse-class-x-board/geography-syllabus ; https://www.boardingschoolsofindia.com/icse-board
- No recent change to the best-of-5-including-English rule found as of 2026 sources.

### ISC Class 12 (VERIFIED by multiple sources; primary CISCE circular not directly retrieved)
- **Aggregate % = (English marks + best 3 other subjects) ÷ 4 × 100** ("Best of 4"; English/Modern English
  compulsory and always counted). No best-of-5 rule for ISC.
  Sources: https://amityonline.com/blog/isc-12th-result-announced ; https://www.kollegeapply.com/exams/isc-board-class-12 ; https://admission.getmyuni.com/article/route?slug=isc-passing-marks
- To qualify for the pass certificate: pass in **English + at least 3–4 other subjects** (five or six
  subjects including English + pass grade in SUPW & Community Service per 2027 rules).
  Source: https://www.kollegeapply.com/exams/isc-board-class-12
- **Division thresholds (VERIFIED, consistent):** Distinction ≥ 75%; First Division 60–74.99%; Pass 35–59.99%.
  Source: https://collegedunia.com/exams/isc/grading-system
- **Pass mark CONFLICTED:** current pages (2025–2026) say **35% per subject** and 35% aggregate;
  several pages still show **40% per subject** (older CISCE rule; pass marks were lowered from 40% to 35%
  for ISC and 40%/33% ICSE effective from the 2024 examinations per board notifications).
  35% sources: https://collegedunia.com/exams/isc/grading-system ; https://degreefyd.com/exams/isc-board-class-12-exam
  40% sources: https://www.collegedekho.com/isc-12th-grading-system-brd ; https://admission.getmyuni.com/article/route?slug=isc-passing-marks
  → For StudyPilot, use **35%** (current rule) and note the older 40% figure appears on outdated pages.
- **Grace marks (secondary source only, UNVERIFIED against CISCE):** "CISCE may award up to 5 grace marks in
  one or more subjects to help a student cross the pass threshold." Source: https://collegedunia.com/exams/isc/grading-system
- **Implication for StudyPilot:** calculator must implement Best-of-5 (ICSE, English forced) and Best-of-4
  (ISC, English forced) — NOT a plain average of all subjects. CISCE official regulations page is
  https://www.cisce.org (could not be fetched by this subagent; verify before launch).

## 3. VTU (Visvesvaraya Technological University)

VTU runs parallel schemes; the formula depends on admission scheme. The split below is corroborated by
4+ independent sources each citing VTU's own documents, and one vtu.ac.in regulation PDF I found
verbatim in search results.

### Current formula — 2021, 2022, 2025 schemes (VERIFIED against vtu.ac.in documents)
- **Percentage of marks secured, M = CGPA Earned × 10.**
- Primary evidence (vtu.ac.in, verbatim): VTU M.Arch Regulations 2024, clause 22-OMA-11.4 — "Percentage of
  marks secured, M = CGPA Earned x 10" (illustration: CGPA 8.20 → M = 8.20 × 10).
  Source: https://vtu.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/03.-M.-Arch_2024-25-Regulation-final-10th-August-2024^.pdf
  (note verbatim filename quirk). Also MCA 2022 regulations at
  https://vtu.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Regulations-Governing-the-award-of-MCA-2022-schem.pdf define
  SGPA/CGPA computation.
- For B.E./B.Tech: Regulations 2022 clause **22OB 6.7** (page 35): "M = CGPA earned × 10"; 2021-scheme
  regulations (effective 2021-22) clause **21OB13.2** (page 37): same formula — as read in the official
  PDFs by two independent secondary sources.
  Sources: https://sgpatopercentage.com/ ; https://toolisky.com/blog/vtu-cgpa-to-percentage-calculator ; https://opencgpa.com/cgpa-to-percentage/ (cites VTU 2021-22, 2022, 2025 UG regulations)

### Older formula — 2015, 2017, 2018 schemes (VERIFIED, VTU's own wording)
- **Percentage = (CGPA − 0.75) × 10** — applies to 2015, 2017 and 2018 schemes ONLY (VTU's own scope wording).
  Source: VTU "CGPA STANDARD FORMULA" page, vtu.ac.in/en/cgpa-standard-formula/ — as cited verbatim by
  https://gradekar.com/cgpa-to-percentage/vtu ; https://toolisky.com/blog/vtu-cgpa-to-percentage-calculator ; https://cashfundamentals.com/cgpa-to-percentage-calculator/
- Effect: 10 CGPA caps at 92.5%; using the wrong scheme shifts the result by 7.5 points.

### CONFLICT found (single-source claim, UNVERIFIED)
- cgpatopercentagetool.com claims a **VTU Notification No. VTU/Exam/Circular/2025/CGPA-PCT dated 17-06-2025**
  prescribing **(CGPA − 0.50) × 10** for the 2021 & 2022 schemes. NO other source corroborates this;
  it contradicts VTU's own 2022 regulations (M = CGPA × 10). Treat as UNVERIFIED — do not implement.
  Source: https://cgpatopercentagetool.com/cgpa-to-percentage-vtu/

### Grade→points table (VTU B.E./B.Tech 2021/2022-era CBCS, corroborated)
- O (90–100)=10, A+ (80–89)=9, A (70–79)=8, B+ (60–69)=7, B (55–59)=6, C (50–54)=5, P (40–49)=4, F (0–39)=0.
  Sources: https://gradekar.com/cgpa-to-percentage/vtu ; https://vtu.ac.in M.Arch/MCA regulation PDFs (O=10 … C=5, F=0 on 10-point scale)
- **Older 2018-era schemes used the letter set S/A/B/C/D/E/F** (S=10, A=9, B=8, C=7, D=6, E=4, F=0).
  Source: https://vtu.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/03.-Mtech-Regulations-2018-19-Corrected-26.06.2020.pdf
- **Pass:** CGPA ≥ 5.00 with no F in any registered course (degree award, M.Arch 2024 wording);
  per-course pass = grade point ≥ pass threshold for the scheme (e.g., ≥ 4/P under newer schemes, ≥ 04/E
  under 2018-era); SGPA/CGPA = Σ(credits × grade points) ÷ Σ(credits).
  Source: VTU regulation PDFs above.
- **Class equivalence, 2021/2022 schemes (corroborated):** First Class with Distinction ≥ 7.00 CGPA
  (and all subjects cleared on first attempt), First Class ≥ 6.00, Second Class ≥ 5.00, Pass ≥ 4.00.
  2018-scheme equivalents: 7.75 / 6.75 / 5.75 / 4.75.
  Source: https://gradekar.com/cgpa-to-percentage/vtu
- **Implication for StudyPilot:** scheme selector is MANDATORY (2021/2022/2025 → ×10; 2015/2017/2018 →
  (CGPA−0.75)×10). VTU grade cards/transcripts show CGPA, not percentage (no separate conversion
  certificate issued by VTU).
  Source: https://gradekar.com/cgpa-to-percentage/vtu

## 4. Mumbai University

MU is the messiest case: four schemes run side-by-side, two have NO formula, and pre-2026 coefficients
are CONFLICTED across secondary sources. I could not retrieve mu.ac.in circulars directly (no live
browser); every claim below names the MU circular as cited by secondary sources.

### Engineering (Faculty of Technology) — formula VERIFIED via transcript evidence
- **% = 7.1 × CGPI + 12, when CGPI < 7; 7.4 × CGPI + 12, when CGPI ≥ 7 — rounded UP to the next whole number.**
- **Circular No. Exam/Engg/720 of 2015, dated 30.11.2015** — printed verbatim on official B.E. transcripts
  (including the round-up instruction). NOT disturbed by the 2026 conversion circular (addressed to
  Arts/Science/Commerce/Management only).
  Sources: https://www.munotes.in/calculators ; https://gradekar.com/blog/cgpa-to-percentage-guide ; https://edvoy.com/articles/mumbai-university-cgpa-to-percentage-converter/
- Note: CGPI = CGPA numerically (same 10-point figure; "Index" vs "Average" is vocabulary: Index under
  Technology/older CBCS, Average under NEP/Arts-Science-Commerce).
  Source: https://www.munotes.in/calculators

### 10-point CBCS (2016-17 → 31.12.2025), pre-NEP general faculties — coefficients CONFLICTED
- Option A: **% = (CGPA × 7.1) + 11** — attributed to Circular Exam/Com/97 of 2018.
  Sources: https://globalcgpa.in/sgpa-to-percentage/ ; https://gradekar.com/blog/cgpa-to-percentage-guide ; https://www.cgpa.app/p/mumbai-university-cgpa-percentage.html ; https://edvoy.com/articles/mumbai-university-cgpa-to-percentage-converter/
- Option B: **% = 7.25 × CGPI + 11, rounded up** — also attributed to Circular Exam/Com/97 of 2018.
  Source: https://www.munotes.in/calculators
- BOTH agree the circular was **repealed effective 1 January 2026** (by Circular Exam/Result/803 of 2026,
  dated 01.01.2026, per resolution of 18.11.2025) and that post-2026 Arts/Science/Commerce/Management use
  **actual marks** (college computes % = total marks obtained ÷ total maximum marks × 100; university
  issues a conversion certificate only on student request).
  Sources: https://www.munotes.in/calculators ; https://gradekar.com/blog/cgpa-to-percentage-guide
  → For StudyPilot: use (CGPA × 7.1) + 11 as the pre-2026 historical figure (most-cited), flag the 7.25
  alternative in the notes, and show NO formula (actual-marks) as the 2026+ rule.

### NEP 2020 (all faculties, newer batches) — no formula (VERIFIED via syllabus circulars, secondary)
- **No equation.** Each NEP syllabus circular prints a band table: e.g., CGPA 7.00–<8.00 → 70–<80%.
  Verified identical across four MU syllabus circulars (Humanities, Commerce, Management).
  Source: https://www.munotes.in/calculators
- NEP grade table (10-point): O 90–100/9.00–10.00/GP10; A+ 80–<90/GP9; A 70–<80/GP8; B+ 60–<70/GP7;
  B 55–<60/5.50–<6.00/GP6; C 50–<55/GP5; P 40–<50/GP4; F <40/GP0.
  Source: https://www.munotes.in/calculators
- **Pass (NEP): 40%, required separately in internal and semester-end paper.**
  Source: https://www.munotes.in/calculators

### CBSGS 7-point (Arts/Science/Commerce UG, 2011-12 → 2016-17) — no formula ever existed
- **Percentage = actual marks obtained.** The 7-point index was never designed to convert; "any site that
  gives you a formula for it is inventing one" (Circular Exam/Com/97 of 2018, paragraph 1).
  Source: https://www.munotes.in/calculators
- 7-point table: O ≥70 marks/GP7; A 60–69.99/GP6; B 55–59.99/GP5; C 50–54.99/GP4; D 45–49.99/GP3;
  E 40–44.99/GP2; F ≤39.99/GP1. Pass = grade E (≥40%).
  Source: https://www.munotes.in/calculators
- **The task's guess "(CGPA − 0.75) × 10 for the 7-point scale" is WRONG for Mumbai University** — that is
  VTU's old formula, misattributed to MU by aggregator sites. Do not implement.
- Gracing (secondary): 1 credit-cum-grade-point added to Σ(credits × GP), never to credits, only to lift
  B→A or A→O (O.5043/O.5044). UNVERIFIED against MU ordinances.
- **Implication for StudyPilot:** MU needs a scheme picker (Engineering / pre-2026 CBCS / NEP-2020 band /
  7-point actual-marks). For any official use, advise requesting the college conversion certificate.

## 5. State-board variance (2–3 concrete examples)
- **Maharashtra State Board (MSBSHSE) SSC/HSC:** purely marks-based — NO CGPA→% conversion exists.
  Percentage = (marks obtained ÷ total maximum) × 100; for SSC the board computes on **best of five**
  subjects (result shows "£" for total/percentage on best five); HSC uses all compulsory subjects.
  Pass: 35% in each subject (theory and practical separately). Divisions: Distinction ≥75%, First Class
  60–74.99%, Second Class 45–59.99%, Pass 35–44.99%. Grace/condonation up to ~15 marks aggregate for
  shortfalls (secondary source).
  Sources: https://news.careers360.com/maharashtra-board-exam-2026-how-ssc-hsc-total-marks-are-calculated-graded-subject-wise-passing-criteria-mahahsscboard-in ; https://www.aubsp.com/maharashtra-hsc-result/ ; https://school.careers360.com/boards/msbshse/maharashtra-board-result
- **General pattern (estimate, UNVERIFIED individually):** most other state boards (UP, Bihar, West Bengal,
  Tamil Nadu, Karnataka SSLC) are likewise direct-marks boards with no GPA conversion — e.g., UP Board
  percentage = total/600 (HS) or /1000 (Intermediate) × 100; West Bengal HS = total/500 × 100. Individual
  board documents were NOT verified in this pass — mark each as UNVERIFIED until checked against the
  board's own publications. The Maharashtra example above is the verified one.
- **Takeaway:** the CGPA→% formula problem is specific to CBSE (legacy), VTU, MU, and other universities;
  state boards generally need only marks→% division plus pass/division thresholds.

## Cross-cutting notes for the StudyPilot grade calculator
1. CBSE: implement ×9.5 ONLY for legacy CCE-era Class 10 marksheets; current marksheets are marks-based —
   show "no conversion needed."
2. ICSE: best-of-5 with English forced. ISC: best-of-4 with English forced.
3. VTU: scheme picker required (2021/2022/2025: ×10; 2015/2017/2018: (CGPA−0.75)×10). Do not use the
   single-source (CGPA−0.50)×10 claim.
4. MU: scheme picker required; post-1-Jan-2026 general faculties = no formula (actual marks);
   engineering formula unchanged (7.1/7.4 × +12). Never (CGPA−0.75)×10 for MU.
5. Never apply CBSE ×9.5 to university CGPAs; never apply any formula to state-board marks.
6. Always cite the formula source and "scheme/batch" in calculator output (UGC requires universities to
   print the rule on the reverse of transcripts).
7. Revaluation effects: a revaluation that changes subject marks re-derives grade points/CGPA and hence
   the converted percentage — but exact board rules (caps, fees, grace-mark limits) were NOT verified in
   this pass: UNVERIFIED for CBSE, CISCE, VTU, MU.
