Four-tool technical specifications

Timetable GeneratorInputs: subjects, hours available per day, school hours, block length. Logic: distributes blocks evenly, rotates subjects, inserts breaks, flags overloaded days. Output: daily + weekly view, printable.
Grade CalculatorInputs: courses, grades, credits, education system. Logic: credit-weighted average using the selected system's documented scale (US 4.0, UK %, India 10-pt CGPA, Nepal 4.0, Singapore 5.0 CAP). Includes a "grade needed" target mode. Every scale is unit-tested against worked examples.
Syllabus TrackerInputs: subjects and topics (manual entry or starter templates). States: not started / learning / revising / mastered. Output: per-subject and overall coverage percentages, gap list. Stored on-device.
Exam CountdownInputs: exam name, date, optional daily study hours. Logic: timezone-safe date math (date-only, no UTC drift), days/weeks remaining, suggested daily revision load. Output: countdown dashboard for multiple exams.

Testing strategy

  • Every calculation tested against hand-worked examples for each grading system
  • Mobile layouts checked at small-phone width first, then tablet and desktop
  • Countdowns verified across timezones and both common date formats (DD/MM and MM/DD)
  • Invalid inputs (empty fields, impossible grades, past dates) handled with clear messages
  • On-device saving tested for save, reload and clear behaviour
  • Keyboard-only and screen-reader pass on every tool before launch

Estimated ongoing maintenance

Static hosting means near-zero upkeep: roughly 0–1 hour per week — checking feedback, adding a grading configuration when students request one, and publishing an occasional study guide. No servers to patch, no database to back up, and $0 running cost on free infrastructure.

Questions

Quick answers

Is StudyPilot really free?

Yes — the core of all four tools is free, with no mandatory registration. If premium extras are ever added later, the core tools students rely on stay free.

Do I need to create an account?

No. Open any tool and start using it immediately. Your plans and progress are kept in your own browser, on your own device.

Does it work with my country's grading system?

At launch: the US (4.0 GPA), UK (percentage and degree classes), India (10-point CGPA), Nepal (NEB 4.0 GPA) and Singapore's NTU/NUS (5.0 CAP), plus percentage-based systems generally. Each scale is separately documented on the methodology page, and new systems are added based on demand.

What happens to my data?

It stays with you. StudyPilot's tools run in your browser and don't send your grades, plans or progress to a server. See the privacy policy in the footer for the full picture.

When do the full tools launch?

On the 30-day plan in the blueprint above: timetable and grade calculator in Week 2, syllabus tracker and countdown in Week 3, and public launch in Week 4 after testing.

About StudyPilot

StudyPilot is a free academic productivity toolkit for high-school and university students worldwide. Our mission is simple: help students organize their academic lives, prepare for exams, and track their progress using tools that are genuinely easy to use.

We started from a pattern every student recognizes — the planning tools that actually help are usually locked behind accounts, paywalls or app installs. StudyPilot takes the opposite approach: free core tools, no mandatory registration, and your data kept on your own device.

The platform is global from day one. Grading systems, calendars and date formats differ around the world, so each education system is a documented, tested configuration — starting with the US, UK, India, Nepal and Singapore, and expanding based on student demand.

  • Grading-scale corrections: include your institution or board name and a link to its official grading table so the configuration can be verified before it's updated.
  • Privacy questions: covered in the Privacy Policy — the short version is that your tool data never leaves your device.

Privacy Policy — the essentials

StudyPilot is designed to collect as little as possible. This summary reflects how the tools are built; the full policy text ships with the public launch.

  • No accounts: there is no registration, so we hold no names, emails or passwords for tool users.
  • Your data stays on your device: timetables, grades, syllabus progress and exam dates entered into the tools are processed in your browser and kept in your browser's own storage. They are not sent to or stored on a StudyPilot server.
  • No sale of data: personal data is never sold or shared for advertising.
  • Clearing your data: clearing your browser's site data removes everything the tools have kept — completely, and at any time.
  • Minimal analytics: if anonymous, aggregate usage counts are ever enabled, they will be disclosed here first, with no cross-site tracking.

Methodology — how calculations work

Grading formulas are not universal, so StudyPilot never applies one country's scale to another's grades. Each system is a separate configuration, documented here and tested against worked examples before release.

  • United States (GPA 4.0): letter grades convert to grade points (A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0), weighted by credit hours.
  • United Kingdom: module marks are credit-weighted percentages; degree classification follows the common bands (First ≥ 70, 2:1 ≥ 60, 2:2 ≥ 50, Third ≥ 40). Individual universities vary, and the tool says so.
  • India (CGPA 10-point): subject grade points (10–5 scale) weighted by credits, averaged to CGPA; percentage conversion uses the board's stated formula where one exists.
  • Nepal (NEB, GPA 4.0): percentage marks map to letter grades and grade points (A+ = 4.0 at 90+, in 10-point bands) per the NEB grading table.
  • Singapore NTU / NUS (CAP 5.0): letter grades convert on the 5.0 scale (A+ / A = 5.0, A− = 4.5, B+ = 4.0 …), weighted by modular credits / AUs.

Countdowns use date-only arithmetic in the student's own timezone, so a day is never lost or gained to UTC conversion. Timetables distribute study blocks evenly across selected subjects and always insert breaks between blocks.

Help & guides

  • Building a timetable that survives real life: start from the hours you truly have, not the hours you wish you had — the generator flags days that are overloaded.
  • Reading your GPA: the calculator shows the formula it used, so you can verify the result against your institution's own table.
  • Using the syllabus tracker: mark a topic "mastered" only after you can solve questions on it without notes — honest ticks make the coverage number useful.
  • Countdown planning: work backwards from the exam date; the planner spreads revision evenly so the final week stays light.
